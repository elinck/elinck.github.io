## New Paper in *Journal of Lost Species*

Back in 2024, Jason and I traveled to the eastern Whiteman Range in West New Britain Province, Papua New Guinea, where we conducted ad-hoc bird surveys and scouted localities for future work. Among the surprises during that trip was a camera trap record of a single New Britain Thrush (*Zoothera talaseae*), then thought to be a ["lost" species](https://searchforlostbirds.org/) with [no documented sightings since 1971](https://www.montana.edu/ecology/msu_researchers_capture_photos_of_new_britain-thrush.html). Correspondence with several other ornithologists working in Melanesia and assessment of their unpublished field notes revealed this was not quite the case: Richard Hazell and Bonny Koane had photographed a mist-netted individual as recently as 2017, while a 1999 expedition by University of Wisconsin and the PNG National Museum and Art Gallery had resulted in three specimens. We thus decided to conduct a systematic review of all known observations of the species, uncovering a mere 15 total (only 11 of which were accompanied by documentation). 

This review---along with conservation implications and suggestions for effective survey methods---has now been published as an open-access paper in the [excellent new *Journal of Lost Species*](https://journaloflostspecies.org/index.php/jls), run out of the University of Nevada Museum of Natural History. You can [view the paper here](https://doi.org/10.66264/3c8z5qxh), or [download a formatted pdf](/static/papers/26_gregg_2026_jls.pdf). (Excitingly, it's my first to have the abstract translated into Tok Pisin.)
